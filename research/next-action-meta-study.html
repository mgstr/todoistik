Next Action Meta Study

A third mark on the Next action heading, beside the pick dot and the check: press it and the project's own meta line is written into the action's. Seven dresses for it, each in the real heading at the real sizes. Every one of them is live — press the mark and watch the box under it fill, then press again to see what a second press does.

The state that decides half of this: with nothing to copy, the app's own rule says the control must not advertise itself.

The options

A ↧

Arrow to a bar

Recommended
Light
Dark

U+21A7, downwards arrow to bar. The bar is the field it lands in, so the glyph says both halves of the act — down, and into this. The app's other arrows all point somewhere else (← → ↳ ↵), so a downward one is unspoken for and cannot be misread as one of them.

It is also the only candidate whose meaning survives the project having two tags rather than one, or a snooze as well — it names the direction, not the cargo.

B ⇣

Double-stroke down arrow

The lighter A
Light
Dark

U+21E3. Reads at a glance and sits at the same weight as ● and ☐ — it is the most restful of the seven on the line. What it gives up is the destination: it says "down" and leaves you to work out down into what, which on a screen with three boxes under the heading is a real question.

C #

The notation itself

Says the cargo, not the act
Light
Dark

A project's meta line holds tags and a snooze and nothing else (design.md, "Writing a project"), so # is literally what is being moved. In monospace it also matches the box it writes into.

Against it: # is a noun where the other two marks are verbs. ● picks and ☐ completes; # says "tag" and leaves the pressing unexplained — and it is the one glyph here that could be read as a filter, which is what # means in the box three lines below it.

D #↓

Both halves, spelled out

Unambiguous, and wide
Light
Dark

Nobody will wonder what it does. But it is two glyphs where every other mark in the app is one, and on a heading it reads as a little word rather than a mark — which puts it in competition with "Next action" for the same glance.

E ⧉

The copy glyph

Borrowed convention
Light
Dark

U+29C9. Two overlapping squares is the copy icon everywhere else on a computer, so it arrives already meaning something. The trouble is that what it already means is "copy to the clipboard", and this control does not — and it sits one glyph away from ☐, which is also a square. At 1rem the two are easy to confuse, and one of them completes the action.

F m

The letter it is pressed with

No glyph to learn
Light
Dark

Honest about being a keyboard app, and it teaches its own shortcut. But the key bar already says m meta at the foot of the screen, and keys.md is firm that a control declares its letter and the mode supplies the chord — a letter printed on a control would be the one place a chord is written into the markup, and it would read wrong the moment keys.mode is modifier.

G #car

The tags themselves, pressable

The one non-icon option
Light
Dark

Not an icon: a dashed chip carrying the line it would copy, in the app's own badge shape. It answers the question every icon above leaves open — what would this copy? — before the press rather than after, and the dashes are the same "nothing about this is saved yet" the draft rows wear.

It costs width, and the width grows with the project's tags. It also stops being a mark in a row of marks, which is what you asked for. It is here because if the answer to "what does ↧ do" turns out to be "you have to remember", this is the option that never needed remembering.

A (↧), with G as the fallback. It is a verb like the two marks beside it, it names the direction rather than the cargo so it stays true when a project carries two tags and a snooze, and no other arrow in the app points down.

If the doubt is whether an arrow can be guessed at all, G is the option that removes the guess — but it is a chip and not a mark, and you asked for a mark.

What the key does

Bare m is free. The only m in the map is ^m, the jump prefix, and keys.md's globals are always ctrl in every mode — so m and ^m are the same legal pair as o and ^o. It is the first letter of the thing it presses, which is what makes a letter guessable, and Meta is a noun no other letter is spent on.

It belongs to the row, not the screen: the heading is the next action's own row, so m would sit beside d and t and fire on the selected row, and the bar would offer it only where the control is drawn — which is why the state in the toggle above matters.

Three things the icon does not settle

Each of these changes what gets built, and none of them is an icon question:

  • A second press. Try pressing any mark above twice. Does the line get the tags once and stop, or does it toggle them back off the way t and r go both ways? A one-way press is the simpler rule; a two-way one is what every other mark on a row does.
  • What happens to what is already in the box. The specimens merge — the project's tags are added to whatever the action's line already says, and a tag already there is not written twice. Replacing the line outright is the other answer, and it would throw away a context or a size that the project has no opinion about.
  • The snooze. A project's meta line may carry snooze:friday, and on an action a snooze means "not workable yet" — copying one down would park the very action the project is waiting on. The specimens copy tags only and leave the snooze behind.

And one that is not a question: nothing is written until Save. The boxes belong to the project's form, so the mark fills the line and the page's one Save commits it — which is also what makes a wrong press cost nothing.
