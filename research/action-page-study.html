Five ways to shorten the edit-action screen

The same four fields, the same two dates and the same seven buttons, laid out five ways. Nothing is removed: every variant below carries 4 fields · 2 dates · 7 buttons, and each is rendered with style.css itself rather than a mockup of it, in both themes.

The heights in the table are measured off this page after it loads, not estimated. They are the height of main's content — the rail, the title bar and the key bar are constant chrome and are left out of every frame.

Ages off is the deciding test for C, D and E — it is the moment their merged line has to survive on its own.

The constraints every variant keeps

  • The dates read last, over the buttons. Decided two commits ago — an action is opened to change its title, and a screen that opens with two timestamps has put its least useful line where the eye lands first. No variant moves the button row to the top.
  • Save stands in the row with Complete and Delete, via the form attribute (implementation.md, "Writing an action"). One row of everything the screen can do. Only D argues with this, and says so.
  • The project is shown, not chosen. Moving an action between projects is Detach and Attach. The box is readonly — which is why C, D and E are free to draw it as the chip it already is on every list row.
  • Four screens share actionfields — the action's page, the processing screen, the add-action dialog, the box under a project's action list. A variant that edits the partial changes all four; a variant that only edits action.html changes one. Each card says which.
  • No labels that instruct. Nothing below adds a word of explanation; two variants remove some.

Where the height actually goes

Measured off A on this page, so it moves when the stylesheet does. Two thirds of the screen is four label words, one box that is five rows whether or not there is anything in it, and one field that cannot be typed into. That is what decides which variants below are worth their cost — and it is why the empty half of the column turns out not to be the problem.

The variants

What I would take

C, then B if it is still too tall. C is the only one that pays for itself without touching a shared partial or a written decision: the project box was never a control, and the dates line was already a row of muted facts about this action, so the chip goes where it belongs rather than where there was room. B is the honest second course — it is four lines of pure margin, and its whole cost is one <span> in a partial four screens read.

D is the variant I expected to win, and it does not. The form is capped at 34rem inside a 62rem column, so nearly half the page sits empty for its whole height, and filling that half looks like the obvious fix. The ledger above says it is not: the height is in the four label words and the description box, not in the empty column, and a rail holding seven buttons is itself nearly as tall as the form beside it. It buys less than C and spends a written decision to do it. Take D if the two-column reading is what you want — not for the height.

E is a question, not a proposal. It is B and C together with the four field labels deleted. On a screen its only user specified and opens several times a day, "Title" over the title box is the same kind of sentence as the placeholder inside it. If that reads as right, E is the answer; if a bare box is one ambiguity too many, C stands.

One thing noticed on the way past

The title box carries placeholder="Starts with a verb, fully self-descriptive", and the project form carries "A reference to the outcome, not what to do". implementation.md's "neither box carries a placeholder" is about the meta and description boxes, so these two are not strictly a contradiction — but they are the same sentence the rule was written against, read for the hundredth time by the person who wrote the rule. It costs no height, so it is not part of any variant above; it is a one-line deletion whenever you want it.
