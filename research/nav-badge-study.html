Nav Badge Study

Four placements for the per-view item counts, rendered with the app's own stylesheet in both of its themes. Counts on Today through Review are stand-ins — only the Inbox has real data yet.

The jump letters occupy the same strip of space. Turn them on to see the conflict.
A

Inline pill — as built today

The one you don't like
Dark
Light

The count sits on the label's baseline, so it reads as a second word in the view's name rather than as a marker attached to it. It also pushes every following label sideways: the bar is at its widest here, and each label's position shifts as counts change.

B

Filled corner badge

The literal iPhone badge
Dark
Light

Solid accent circle, white number, Inbox in red. Unmistakable, and the badge leaves the text flow so labels sit at even intervals. The cost is volume: ten filled dots is a lot of colour in a bar that is on screen constantly, and it spends the accent on counts rather than on the active view.

C

Bare superscript

Typography, not chrome
Dark
Light

No pill at all — a small raised number, muted grey, red for the Inbox. The lightest of the three and the most typographic. But without an enclosing shape the number reads as part of the label at a glance, which is close to the problem in A, and it needs extra right padding to avoid touching the next word.

D

Outlined corner pill

Recommended
Dark
Light

The same corner position as B, in the badge palette the rest of the app already uses. It keeps the enclosing shape that makes a count read as a count, without turning the bar into a field of dots — and it leaves red and the accent free to mean what they already mean here: the Inbox needs emptying, and this is the view you are on.

One thing to settle with it

Turn on the g hints above. The jump letters sit at each item's top-left, and a corner count sits at the previous item's top-right — so in B, C and D they land in the same strip and overlap. Three ways out:

Blank the counts while g is held. During the overlay you are choosing a destination, not reading counts. Costs nothing and no layout moves.

Widen the gap between items. Fixes it permanently, but pays horizontal space always to solve something visible only while a key is held.

Leave it. Both stay legible; it just looks accidental rather than decided.
